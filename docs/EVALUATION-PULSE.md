Between-country distances over all 136 pairs: Pearson correlation between estimated and planted TV = 0.905. Largest planted contrast: DE–GB (planted 0.650, estimated 0.578).

Mean pairwise distance: planted 0.375, estimated 0.337; without the one-day spike 0.350; regenerated without replays (repeat probability 0) and without the spike 0.394. Without the simulated behaviour the mean distance returns to about the planted level; the small remaining difference is sampling noise (the distance between two noisy estimates tends to exceed the distance between the true distributions, more so for countries with few listeners). The reduction seen in the full data therefore comes from the simulated behaviour — replays pick uniformly among a user's previously heard tracks, pulling every country towards the overall mix, and the spike adds the same Ambient listening everywhere. This illustrates that p[r,g] measures event-weighted demand, not latent preference (MATHEMATICS.md section 3).

## Interpretation limits

These results show that the implementation recovers patterns that were deliberately planted by a simple generator with known parameters; they do not show how real listeners behave. The playlist-add signal is omitted (no timestamped add events yet) and its weight is renormalized across the other signals. Precision@K with K = 3 is coarse. Synthetic users choose tracks independently given their taste, which is simpler than real social influence.
